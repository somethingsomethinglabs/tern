# Trailrest naming workshop and research

Research date: 23 September 2026.

Decision: the user selected **Tern** after this workshop and requested the project rebrand. The candidate rankings below record the exploration; they are not the current decision. Domain and trademark availability remain unchecked.

This is a first-pass naming collision screen for the browser described in [README.md](../README.md) and [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md). Its defining promise is that users can put unfinished work aside and resume with their context intact. A name should support that promise without implying that every website's unsaved state is preserved.

## Current direction: short, distinctive browser brand

The user chose this direction after the initial workshop. Prioritise a compact standalone identity over a name that explains pausing and resuming. Foldway and Retrail move out of the leading shortlist because their descriptive compounds fit the earlier brief better.

My new creative favourite is Kest. Avren is the strongest alternative with a softer sound. Tern remains the benchmark from round one. Tovin and Eave offer friendlier alternatives. All five have existing uses; this is a creative shortlist with documented conflicts, not a set of available names.

Pronunciations below are intended brand pronunciations, not claims about established names in other languages. These names need no invented etymology or acronym.

The recommendation is to compare Kest, Avren, and Tern as names before further identity work. My order on creative fit is Kest, Avren, Tern. That order is not an assessment of legal availability. None merits an automatic project rename.

Use the same plain description for every candidate so that the naming comparison stays fair: "A browser that keeps your work in context." Then read "Open it in Kest", "Make Avren your default browser", and "I use Tern" aloud. A listener should be able to repeat the name and have a reasonable chance of spelling it.

### Round-two exclusions and research limits

[Oriel](https://openoriel.com/) and [Veyra](https://docs.veyraprotocol.io/veyra-browser) already name browsers. [Orris](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/orris/jkiealmpcbdjengdjlndcnocoggaalcj) is a reading-analysis extension. [Fenn](https://www.usefenn.com/) is a private AI memory/search app, while [Aven](https://aventasks.dev/) is a task manager. Those are closer category conflicts and were dropped.

[Vireo](https://www.idovio.com/products/vireo/) names a local AI productivity product, and [Vireo Sentinel](https://vireosentinel.com/) is a browser-based governance product. [Veylo](https://veylo.lol/how-it-works.html) is a browser communication extension. Other names screened but not advanced include Brio, Elvo, Sova, Nivo, Orvo, Rusk, Virel, Rovik, Tavri, Orrel, Skern, Kivren, Elvori, and Tavren. The additional research pass also screened Dunlin, Siskin, Dovra, Orven, Selvo, Vaelo, Tovan, Wold, and Thirl. These do not all have direct browser conflicts; they lost on the combined judgment of naming appeal and existing use.

Round-two searches combined each name with browser, software, app, and technology. Broader follow-ups mattered: the first Avren results were dominated by a font, while a targeted software query found actual software businesses. No browser result is evidence only about the queries reviewed, never proof that a name is unused. No systematic trademark, domain, or handle checks have been completed.

## Initial recommendation

Workshop Tern, Foldway, and Retrail first. Tern is my creative favourite, but it has substantial existing software use. Foldway is the more promising candidate for a further availability check. Retrail is the strongest evolution of the current name. These are options for discussion, not a selected or cleared brand.

I would change Trailrest if one of these directions appeals. Trailrest is pronounceable and expresses permission to pause. Its weakness is that it sounds like outdoor accommodation or camping equipment, while the product's useful distinction is continuing work. That outdoor association is also present in actual use: Chinook lists a [Trailrest Double-Wide sleeping mat](https://www.chinooktec.com/product.php?act=list&cid=3). This is a different product category, not evidence of a browser conflict.

The naming brief is a browser that helps you keep your place across tasks. Prefer a name that is comfortable in "Open it in ___" and "Make ___ your default browser", easy to say, and broad enough to cover ordinary browsing. Avoid making automatic saving, perfect memory, or AI central to the name. The [design brief](design-brief.md) and [proposed AI direction](practical-ai-direction.md) do not support those as universal promises.

Suggested copy for hearing the names in context:

- "Tern. Pick up where you left off."
- "Foldway. A browser for work you come back to."
- "Retrail. Return to the task, with your next step in view."

These are draft positioning lines. They do not mean arbitrary unsaved website state survives closing or restarting the browser. Do not rename the app's Task, Later, or Settled concepts merely to match a naming metaphor.

For the next workshop, compare Tern's short, independent identity with Foldway's put-aside metaphor and Retrail's return-to-work meaning. Saying each name aloud in an ordinary recommendation is more useful than inventing a numerical brand score. Before a public choice, check the preferred name's domain, handles, and relevant trademark records. Those checks were not part of this pass.

## Implications for the workshop

The strongest meaning is continuity: keeping one's place, leaving a useful marker, and returning without reconstructing the task. Nature and shelter metaphors can support it, but a pleasant short noun is insufficient. Cairn and Cove demonstrate how readily other products reach the same metaphors.

Names built from less obvious combinations deserve a pass before choosing a familiar single word. A compound can retain the meaning while becoming easier to search. This is a direction for generating candidates, not evidence that a particular compound is available.

Avoid making AI the main naming idea. The project's work-preservation promise remains useful with AI off, while names such as Tandem already place human and AI collaboration at the centre of their identity.

## Method and limits

The comparison table below Findings covers 13 initial candidates. The workshop and additional screening sections expand that set. Searches combined candidate names with browser, tabs, tab manager, productivity, app, software, and official, with broad exact-name searches for several finalists. Key shortlist queries included `"Tern" "web browser"`, `"Tern" browser tabs sessions`, `"Foldway"`, `"Foldway" browser software app`, `"Retrail"`, `"Retrail" software app`, `"Thence" browser`, `"Thence" app`, and `"Trailrest"`. Evidence comes from product owners' sites, their source repositories, and publisher-authored app-store descriptions. Product descriptions establish existing naming and positioning; this research did not test those products' functional claims.

The two Apple listings were readable through search results, but direct page opens returned a cache error. Treat them as indexed publisher listings pending a live manual recheck. Cairn's other close collisions have independent sources.

No trademark-register, domain-registration, or social-handle availability checks were performed. Search absence would not establish availability, and this bounded pass does not establish that an exact browser name is absent. No project rename was made.
